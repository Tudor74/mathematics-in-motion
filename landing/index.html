In Motion

Ideas you can play, pause, rewind and try

In Motion is a family of visual courses. Each idea gets a scene: a plain definition, an example, and a diagram that moves. Play it like a film, rewind to the moment that confused you, then take the controls and try it yourself.

What “in motion” means

  • One idea per sceneA term, a plain-English definition, an example and one diagram, so nothing competes for attention.
  • The same moment, the same pictureEach diagram is drawn from the lesson clock. Pause, rewind or replay and you see exactly what you saw before.
  • Hold to rewindIn every course, the speed slider runs backwards while held and pauses when released. Forward speed stays where you set it.
  • Try itThe same diagram responds to your input: flip bits, take part in experiments, choose behind Rawls’s veil, or sweep a filter and hear it.
  • Words that explain themselvesUnderlined words open a short meaning, a distinction and an example.
  • One page eachEach course is a single web page that runs in your browser, with nothing to install and no account to make.

Where to start? Each course stands on its own. If you want an order, Mathematics gives the language, Computer Science shows how machines use it, HCI shows how people meet those machines, Philosophy asks what all of it means, Therapies turns to how people help one another, Electronic Music is where the maths becomes something you can dance to, Awais Rashid In Motion follows one researcher across a quarter-century, and The Future looks ahead to 2040.

The courses share one visual language: large touch targets, keyboard controls, a caption under every diagram, respect for reduced motion, and en-GB spelling.
